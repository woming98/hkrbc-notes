§1 Framework Overview 框架總覽

HKRBC (Hong Kong Risk-Based Capital) came into force on 1 July 2024 alongside the Insurance (Amendment) Ordinance 2023. The framework uses an Economic Balance Sheet (EBS 經濟資產負債表) as its foundation. The Prescribed Capital Amount (PCA 訂明資本額) Cap. 41R Rule 37 is determined via stress-scenario methods across four risk modules, and compared against the available Capital Base (資本基礎) Cap. 41R Rule 7 to establish capital adequacy per Rule 5.

§3 Matching Adjustment (MA) 匹配調整 Cap. 41R Rule 24

MA is an additional spread applied to the risk-free discount rate for qualifying MA portfolios (官方 Cap. 41R 術語 Rule 2: "MA portfolio"). It allows a portion of the excess asset spread (above credit risk) to enhance the liability discount rate, thereby reducing the current estimate (現時估計值; 非 Cap. 41R 術語的 "BEL") and releasing capital.

§3.1 Adjusted Spread (ADD) 調整後利差 Cap. 41R Sch. 5

Step 1: Asset Spread per bond 資產利差 Sch. 5 §§2, 5

Official Cap. 41R term: "asset spread" (資產利差) — defined in Schedule 5 §2 as "the constant spread that makes the price of an eligible asset equal to the present value of its cash flows when added to the specified risk-free yield curve at each point on the spot rate." In standard financial practice this is commonly called the Z-Spread. The value \(z\) is solved numerically:

$$Price_i = \sum_{t} \frac{CF_t}{(1 + r_t^{RFR} + z)^t} \quad \xrightarrow{\text{solve for}} \quad z = \text{asset spread}_i$$

Step 2: Asset Duration & Asset Dollar Duration 資產存續期 Sch. 5 §§2, 8

Official Cap. 41R term: "duration" (存續期) — defined in Schedule 5 §2 as "a measure of sensitivity of the value of asset or liability with respect to a change in interest rate … percentage change in price of the asset or value of the liability for a 100 basis point change in yield." In standard practice this corresponds to the ESD (Effective Spread Duration) for spread-sensitive assets.

$$\text{asset dollar duration} = \text{weighted average duration of eligible assets} \times MV_{\text{eligible assets}}$$ $$\text{liability dollar duration} = \text{effective duration of liabilities} \times CE_{\text{liabilities discounted at RFR}+MA_{est}}$$

Note: "asset dollar duration" and "liability dollar duration" are the official Schedule 5 §8 terms. These correspond to what is commonly called "dollar duration" or "DV01 × notional" in practice. Asset duration measures sensitivity to a 100 bp parallel shift in spread; it is used as the weighting metric in MA.

Step 3: Risk Correction & Adjusted Spread per asset Sch. 5 §5 Table 4 (RC%) & §4 Table 2 (Maximal Make-Whole Call Spread)

Risk correction removes the portion of asset spread attributable to expected credit losses. The official Cap. 41R formula from Schedule 5 §5(2): adjusted spread = Σ ωi × (spreadi − RCi), where RCi is the risk correction percentage.

$$\text{adjusted spread}_i = \text{asset spread}_i \times (1 - RC_i\%)$$

For assets with make-whole call options (official Cap. 41R term per Sch. 5 §4), the asset spread is capped at the maximal make-whole call spread before applying risk correction. Note: "MWC" used informally; official terms are "make-whole call option" and "make-whole call spread."

Step 4: Portfolio-level weighted average adjusted spread Sch. 5 §§5, 9–11

The portfolio adjusted spread is the duration-weighted average across all eligible assets. The official weight ωi per Schedule 5 §5(2)(a) is based on the proportion of each asset group's market value multiplied by its asset duration:

$$\omega_i = \frac{MV_i \times \text{asset duration}_i}{\displaystyle\sum_j MV_j \times \text{asset duration}_j}$$ $$ADD_{portfolio} = \sum_i \omega_i \times ADD_i$$

§3.2 Application Ratio (AR) 應用比率 Cap. 41R Sch. 5 §§12–28

Predictability Factor (PF) 可預測性因子 Sch. 5 §§12–18

PF (predictability factor) measures how reliably asset cash flows can cover liability outflows even under extreme lapse scenarios. It is derived from the accumulated cash flow shortfall percentage (ACS%, official Cap. 41R term per Sch. 5 §7):

$$PF = \max\!\left(0,\ 1 - ACS\%_{max}\right)$$ $$ACS\%_{max} = \max\!\left(ACS\%_{Base},\ ACS\%_{Lapse\ Up},\ ACS\%_{Mass\ Lapse}\right)$$

ACS% for each scenario is computed by rolling forward a cumulative surplus/deficit account year by year:

$$Excess_t = Excess_{t-1} + Asset\_CF_t - Liab\_Net\_CF_t + Interest_{t-1}$$ $$Interest_{t-1} = \max(Excess_{t-1},\ 0) \times r_t^{RFR} \quad \text{(positive balance earns interest only)}$$ $$PV\_Shortfall_t = \begin{cases} |Excess_t| \times \displaystyle\prod_{s=1}^{t}\frac{1}{1+r_s} & \text{if } Excess_t < 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$ $$ACS\%_{scenario} = \frac{\displaystyle\max_{t=1}^{LLP}\ PV\_Shortfall_t}{MV_{Eligible\ Assets}}$$
Lapse Scenario 情景Liability Cash Flow Impact 負債端影響
BaseBest estimate lapse rates 最佳估計退保率
Lapse UpLapse rate × 1.40 (+40%); surrender benefit outflows increase
Mass Lapse30% of individual policies lapse in Year 1 (one-off shock)

Key constraints: Only cash flows within the last liquid point (LLP) Sch. 5 §7(6); Sch. 4 are considered. The last liquid point is the longest point at which the specified risk-free yield curve is liquid (per Sch. 5 §7(6) and Sch. 4). Liability outflows = guaranteed benefits only (future discretionary benefits / TVOG excluded); net of premium income. Asset cash flows are the same across all three scenarios (buy-and-hold strategy assumed).

Simplified Proxy for Lapse Scenarios Rule 24(6)(b) IA Circular 12 Jun 2024, App. 2(b)

$$ACS\%_{Lapse\ Up} = ACS\%_{Base} + 25\% \quad\quad ACS\%_{Mass\ Lapse} = ACS\%_{Base} + 25\%$$

Avoids running stressed actuarial models for lapse scenarios; subject to IA approval.

Duration Factor (DF) 久期因子 Sch. 5 §8

DF compares the asset dollar duration of eligible assets against the liability dollar duration of insurance liabilities subject to MA (official Cap. 41R terms per Sch. 5 §8):

$$\text{asset dollar duration} = \text{weighted average duration (eligible assets)} \times MV_{\text{eligible assets}}$$ $$\text{liability dollar duration} = \text{effective duration (liabilities)} \times CE_{\text{liabilities at RFR+MA}_{est}}$$ $$DF = \min\!\left(\text{eligible asset percentage},\ \frac{\text{asset dollar duration}}{\text{liability dollar duration}}\right); \quad \text{floored at 0}$$

Where eligible asset percentage (official Sch. 5 §8 term) = portion of eligible assets to total invested assets, capped at 100%. DF is floored at 0.

Application Ratio 應用比率 Sch. 5 §6

$$AR = PF \times DF \quad \text{(i.e. predictability factor × duration factor; both per Sch. 5 §6)}$$

Simplified Approach (a) Rule 24(6)(a): AR = 15% flat at entity level, no constant prescribed spread component recognised.

Constant Prescribed Spread Component 固定訂明利差組成部分 Cap. 41R Sch. 5 §9 Table 5 (CPS rates)

The constant prescribed spread component (official Cap. 41R term per Sch. 5 §9; informally abbreviated "CPS") compensates for the "duration gap" — the portion of eligible assets whose duration cannot be matched to liabilities. It applies to all MA portfolios (Par, Non-Par, Capital) for investment-grade eligible assets only. Sovereign bonds of qualifying jurisdictions: constant prescribed spread = 0 (per Sch. 5 §9(3)).

$$CPS\ Part = Weighted\ CPS_{portfolio} \times PF \times \max\!\left[\min\!\left(20\%,\ EA\% - \frac{ADD}{LDD}\right),\ 0\right]$$

where \(EA\%\) = eligible assets as a proportion of total invested assets; the gap \(EA\% - ADD/LDD\) is capped at 20% and floored at 0.

If \(ADD_{portfolio} < Weighted\ CPS\), substitute \(ADD_{portfolio}\) for \(Weighted\ CPS\).

Qualified LTA 合格長期調整 Cap. 41R Sch. 5 §§29–35

Additional adjustment for qualifying portfolios (physically segregated participating or universal life business) to reflect the liquidity premium of long-term equity and property holdings:

$$Qualified\ LTA = LTA_{prescribed} \times \min\!\left(\frac{MV_{Equity+Property}}{MV_{Total\ Invested\ Assets}},\ Cap_{30y+}\right)$$ $$Cap_{30y+} = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{t>30} PV(Liability\ CF_t)}{\displaystyle\sum_{all\ t} PV(Liability\ CF_t)}$$

\(LTA_{prescribed} = 100\ bps\). The equity/property proportion is capped by the weight of liabilities with duration > 30 years, preventing over-recognition.

Note 備註 Qualified LTA is typically set to zero if the portfolio does not meet the physical segregation requirement.

Dynamic MA for Credit Spread Stress 信用利差壓力下的動態匹配調整 Sch. 5 Table 4 (RF)

When credit spreads widen, Z-Spreads increase, causing ADD to rise — but this increase must be partially offset by a Reduction Factor (RF) (a regulatory haircut) to avoid over-recognition of stressed spread in the discount rate.

$$MA_{CS\ stressed} = AR \times \left[ADD_{base} + RF \times \left(ADD_{stressed} - ADD_{base}\right)\right] + CPS\ Part_{stressed}$$

Net ΔNAVIR stress ≈ ( Durationassets − Durationliabilities ) × ΔRate × Portfolio Value
If asset duration = liability duration → Net ΔNAV ≈ 0 → IR PCR ≈ 0

§8.2 Credit Spread Risk & Dynamic MA 信用利差風險與動態匹配調整

Credit Spread (CS) stress under Rule 48 widens all eligible asset spreads. Crucially, under Rule 46, MA is recalculated under the CS stress — so the liability discount rate also falls, partially offsetting the asset value decline. This "Dynamic MA" effect is the key ALM benefit:

Dynamic MA under CS Stress:
Spread widening → asset prices fall ↓ (negative) & MA also rises ↑ (positive for liabilities)
Net PCRCS = ΔNAV(assets) − ΔNAV(liabilities with stressed MA)
The better the ALM match, the more the Dynamic MA offsets CS stress → lower CS PCR

def ma_engine(assets, liabilities, scenarios, tol_bps=10, max_iter=20):
    # --- Step 1-2: Asset Side ---
    for a in assets.eligible:
        a.adj_spread = a.asset_spread * (1 - RC_TABLE[a.rating_band])
    w = np.array([a.mv * a.duration for a in assets.eligible])
    w /= w.sum()
    port_adj_spread = (w * np.array([a.adj_spread for a in assets.eligible])).sum()
    
    # --- Step 3-4: PF (only computed once, scenario CFs are fixed) ---
    acs = {scen: compute_acs(assets, liabilities[scen]) for scen in scenarios}
    pf = max(0, 1 - max(acs.values()))
    
    # --- Step 5-9: Fixed-point iteration on MA ---
    ma_old = port_adj_spread * 0.5  # initial guess
    for it in range(max_iter):
        l_dollar_dur = liabilities.base.dollar_duration(discount_rate=RFR + ma_old)
        a_dollar_dur = sum(a.mv * a.duration for a in assets.eligible)
        ea_pct = sum(a.mv for a in assets.eligible) / assets.invested_mv
        df = max(0, min(ea_pct, a_dollar_dur / l_dollar_dur))
        ar = pf * df
        
        cps_weighted = sum(w[i] * (CPS_TABLE[a.bucket][a.rating_band] - RC_TABLE[a.rating_band])
                           for i, a in enumerate(assets.eligible))
        cps_comp = cps_weighted * pf * (a_dollar_dur / l_dollar_dur)
        
        lta = 100 if assets.qualifies_lta else 0
        lta *= min(assets.equity_proportion, assets.lta_cap)
        
        ma_new = ar * port_adj_spread + cps_comp + lta
        if abs(ma_new - ma_old) < tol_bps:
            return ma_new
        ma_old = ma_new
    raise ConvergenceError("MA did not converge within tolerance")

Calibration Methodology 校準方法

The ESG must be simultaneously market-consistent and arbitrage-free. Calibration is typically a 4-step process:

  1. Yield Curve Bootstrapping: Build the RFR curve from swap quotes / government bonds (Sch. 4 Table 1 prescribed curves for HKD, USD, RMB, etc.). Apply Smith-Wilson or Nelson-Siegel-Svensson interpolation beyond Last Liquid Point.
  2. Cross-Asset Correlation: Estimate covariance matrix from historical data; apply Cholesky decomposition L LT = Σ. Use correlated standard normals Z* = L · Z to drive correlated Brownian motions.
  3. Parameter Calibration: Minimise sum of squared errors between model-implied prices and market prices: $$\min_{\theta} \sum_k \left( P_k^{model}(\theta) - P_k^{market} \right)^2$$ Typically calibrated separately per asset class, then validated for cross-asset consistency.
  4. Scenario Generation: Monte Carlo simulation, typically 5,000-10,000 scenarios × 100 years × monthly time steps. Use antithetic variates and low-discrepancy sequences (Sobol) for variance reduction.

§9.6 TVOG Calculation Deep-Dive 時間價值深度計算

TVOG (Time Value of Options and Guarantees, 選擇權與保證的時間價值) is one of the most technically demanding calculations under HKRBC. Cap. 41R does not use the "TVOG" abbreviation directly — the official term is "valuation of contractual options and financial guarantees" per Rule 19. The standard valuation methodology is the stochastic−deterministic difference approach.

§9.6.1 Core Definition & Mathematical Foundation 核心定義與數學基礎

TVOG = CEstochastic − CEdeterministic
where CE = current estimate (現時估計值) per Rule 16
  • Deterministic CE (確定式現時估計值): PV of cash flows under a single best-estimate path — the "expected" scenario.
  • Stochastic CE (隨機式現時估計值): Arithmetic mean of PV of cash flows across N ≥ 1,000 risk-neutral economic scenarios.

Why TVOG ≠ 0 — Jensen's Inequality

The root cause of non-zero TVOG is asymmetric payoff from policyholder options and insurer guarantees. By Jensen's inequality, for a convex payoff function f(X):

E[f(X)] ≠ f(E[X])
Deterministic CE = f(E[X]);   Stochastic CE = E[f(X)]
→ TVOG = E[f(X)] − f(E[X]) ≥ 0  (typically positive — a cost to the insurer)

Intuitive Example: Universal Life with 2% Minimum Crediting Rate

Investment Return ScenarioActual Crediting RateInsurer Cost from Guarantee
+5% (good scenario)5% (no floor triggered)0 (guarantee out-of-the-money)
+1% (moderate adverse)2% (floor triggered)+1% (insurer absorbs 1%)
−2% (severe adverse)2%+4% (insurer absorbs 4%)

Under deterministic best-estimate (e.g. 4%), the guarantee never triggers → cost = 0. Under stochastic with say 30% of scenarios having return < 2%, the average guarantee cost is significantly positive. This difference is TVOG.

                       + E[ value of early exercise (surrender option on AS) ]   (American option — TVOG surrender)
TVOGPar = Liability ValuePar − Deterministic CE

This decomposition is powerful because it maps cleanly onto closed-form approximations (Black-Scholes put for the guarantee, Bermudan put for the surrender option), allowing fast TVOG estimation when full stochastic runs are infeasible.

§9.7.5 Stochastic-on-Stochastic (Nested MC) 雙重隨機

When Required: If management action depends on current fair value (not just current earned rate), the inner loop must re-value liability at every outer point — leading to nested MC (1,000 × 1,000 = 1,000,000 simulations per policy).

Common workarounds to avoid nested MC computational explosion:

  • Least-Squares Monte Carlo (LSMC, Longstaff-Schwartz): Regress continuation values on state variables; replaces inner MC with polynomial fit. Industry standard for VA hedging and complex Par.
  • Replicating Portfolio (RP): Find a portfolio of vanilla options that replicates liability cash flows. Once calibrated, RP can be valued in closed form at any state.
  • Proxy Models / Curve Fitting / Gaussian Process Regression: Build a fast surrogate for inner valuation using ML techniques.

§9.7.7 Asset-Liability Feedback Loop 資產負債回饋循環

Par fund TVOG calculation has a unique feedback loop not present in other product TVOG calculations. The chain runs as follows in each stress scenario: 

  IR Down stress
    → Asset value ↑ (bonds rally)
    → Guaranteed liability ↑ (lower discount rate)
    → Fund surplus change is ambiguous
    → Affects current year's dividend declaration
    → Affects subsequent RB / TB pattern
    → Affects policyholder surrender behaviour (via DPB)
    → Feeds back to asset rebalancing decision
    → Asset duration shifts
    → Repeats next year

This feedback must be modelled sequentially within each scenario — no shortcuts allowed. Locking dividends at base-case levels and running stress separately leads to material TVOG misstatement.

§9.7.9 Simplified Python Implementation 簡化Python實作

import numpy as np

def par_fund_tvog(scenarios, policy, smoothing_alpha=0.3,
                  rb_share=0.4, tb_share=0.6, beta1=2.0, beta2=1.5):
    """Compute Par fund TVOG (simplified illustration).
    
    scenarios:  np.array shape (N, T), per-year earned rate
    policy:     dict with SA, guaranteed_rate, base_lapse, qx[], etc.
    """
    N, T = scenarios.shape
    pv_per_scenario = np.zeros(N)
    
    for s in range(N):
        # Initialize
        asset = policy['initial_asset']
        vested_rb = 0  # cumulative RB (vested → guaranteed)
        smoothed_rate = scenarios[s, 0]
        last_inforce = 1.0
        cf_per_year = np.zeros(T)
        
        for t in range(T):
            earned_t = scenarios[s, t]
            
            # ─── Smoothing ───
            smoothed_rate = (smoothing_alpha * earned_t
                             + (1 - smoothing_alpha) * smoothed_rate)
            
            # ─── Dividend declaration (with cap/floor) ───
            target_div = max(smoothed_rate - policy['guaranteed_rate'], 0)
            target_div = min(target_div, policy['div_cap'])
            
            # ─── Split into RB (vested) and TB (discretionary) ───
            rb_rate_t = rb_share * target_div
            tb_rate_t = tb_share * target_div
            vested_rb += rb_rate_t * policy['SA']
            
            # ─── DPB with TB anticipation ───
            rate_gap = max(scenarios[s, t] - smoothed_rate, 0)
            tb_decline = max((tb_rate_t - tb_share * target_div), 0)  # proxy
            lapse_t = policy['base_lapse'] * (1 + beta1 * rate_gap + beta2 * tb_decline)
            
            # ─── Cash flows ───
            death_outflow = (policy['SA'] + vested_rb) * policy['qx'][t]
            surr_outflow = ((policy['SA'] * policy['guaranteed_cv_pct']
                             + vested_rb
                             + tb_rate_t * policy['SA'])
                            * lapse_t)
            
            cf_t = -(death_outflow + surr_outflow) * last_inforce
            cf_per_year[t] = cf_t
            
            last_inforce *= (1 - policy['qx'][t] - lapse_t)
            asset = asset * (1 + earned_t) + cf_t
        
        # ─── Discount using scenario's risk-free path ───
        df = np.cumprod(1 / (1 + scenarios[s, :]))
        pv_per_scenario[s] = np.sum(cf_per_year * df)
    
    ce_stochastic = pv_per_scenario.mean()
    
    # Deterministic CE: use mean path (single run)
    mean_path = scenarios.mean(axis=0, keepdims=True)
    ce_deterministic, _, _ = par_fund_tvog(mean_path, policy, smoothing_alpha,
                                            rb_share, tb_share, beta1, beta2)
    
    tvog = ce_stochastic - ce_deterministic
    return ce_stochastic, ce_deterministic, tvog

Note: production code is typically 10-50x more complex (handles multi-currency, cohort segmentation, full asset modelling, replicating portfolio shortcuts for nested MC, etc.).

Appendix A: ESG Minimum Requirements Cap. 41R Rule 19(2) IA Circular 12 Jun 2024, Appendix 1

The Economic Scenario Generator (ESG) used in computing TVOG (Time Value of Options and Guarantees) must satisfy the following minimum requirements under Cap. 41R Rule 19(2):

  • Number of economic scenarios: ≥ 1,000
  • Time step: at least annual; more frequent steps required where product features are sensitive to cash flow timing
  • Must consider all material financial risks, including:
    • Interest rate model (at least short, medium and long tenor movements)
    • Fixed income asset return model (function of: level/change of interest rates, credit spreads, credit default, random component)
    • Inflation model (where relevant and material)
    • Equity asset return model
    • Cross-asset correlations 資產類別間相關性
  • Must pass Martingale Test 鞅檢驗 and Market Consistency Test
  • Must pass Implied Volatility Test — simulated asset class volatilities must be consistent with market data
  • Must validate simulated correlations against historical data
  • Calibrated to RFR yield curves prescribed in Cap. 41R Schedules 4 & 5 (with MA if applicable)
  • Must be re-calibrated at least annually 每年重新校準
